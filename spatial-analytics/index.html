Spatial Analytics

The grammar of space, extended by AI.

Space syntax gave architecture a measurable language for movement: integration, choice, isovists, natural movement. We treat that language as the precursor and use AI to read what the graph alone cannot: heat, shade, material, and the street as people actually experience it.

Integration
How connected a space is to all others. The classic predictor of footfall.
Choice
How often a route lies on the shortest paths through the network. Where movement concentrates.
Isovist
What can be seen from a point. The geometry of visibility that guides orientation.
The Residual
The share of movement the graph cannot see. Our research and design target.

Configuration first: the space syntax core

Bill Hillier's insight still holds: layout itself is the primary generator of movement. We build that baseline rigorously before any AI enters the pipeline.

Network analysis

Axial and segment models of buildings, campuses, and streets, computed in DepthmapX and Grasshopper based workflows.

  • Axial maps and segment angular analysis
  • Integration and choice at multiple radii
  • Visual graph analysis and isovist fields

Natural movement, measured

Configuration predicts a majority of pedestrian flows. We benchmark model output against observed movement so the numbers stay honest.

  • Gate counts and observation protocols
  • Correlation benchmarks against predictions
  • Clear reporting of what the model explains and what it misses

Where the graph goes blind, AI keeps reading

An axial map is identical with or without trees, arcades, or afternoon heat. Whatever those do to movement lands in the residual. That is exactly where we point the machine learning.

Environmental coupling

Microclimate variables joined to the spatial graph, so thermal comfort becomes a first-class spatial parameter.

  • Thermal comfort mapping (PET) and radiation studies
  • Shading geometry, canopy, and arcade conditions
  • Sky view factor and urban heat island metrics

Headed AI

Machine learning enlarges the variable set and tests design options underneath, while every decision stays verifiable in Rhino and Revit.

  • Learning models on enriched spatial plus environmental data
  • Generative options in Dynamo and Grasshopper
  • Architect-verified outputs, never black box hand-offs
